Background checks, explained simply

A background check is inexpensive peace of mind when you're bringing someone into your loved one's home. Here's what it covers, what it can't tell you, and how to use it fairly.

What a good check includes

  • Identity verification, confirming they are who they say.
  • A national criminal database search.
  • A sex-offender registry search.
  • County court records where they've lived, the most reliable source, since serious records live at the county level.
  • A driving record, if they'll be driving your loved one.

A check should be renewed periodically (yearly is common), because a one-time check only reflects that moment in time.

The honest part: no background check, on any service, can guarantee safety. Databases have gaps, county records vary, and a clean result means nothing was found, not that nothing exists. Treat a check as one important layer, alongside a real interview, reference calls, a trial shift, and staying involved.

Consent and fair use

Two things to know so you handle this properly:

  • Consent is required. You need the caregiver's written permission before running a check.
  • If results affect your decision, fair-use rules (the federal FCRA) generally require certain steps, like telling the person and giving them a chance to respond, before you decline based on what a report shows. A reputable screening service guides you through this.

Also fair to remember: an old, minor, or unrelated record isn't automatically a reason to say no. Look at what genuinely affects safety and trust.

Common questions

What does a caregiver background check include?

Identity, a national criminal database, the sex-offender registry, and county court records, plus a driving record if they'll drive. Renew it periodically.

Can it guarantee safety?

No. It's one important layer, not a guarantee. Pair it with interviews, references, a trial shift, and staying involved.

Do I need their permission?

Yes, written consent. And if results affect your decision, follow fair-use (FCRA) steps. A good screening service helps you do this right.

This is general information, not legal advice. Background-check laws and fair-use requirements vary, when in doubt, use a reputable screening service or consult a professional.
