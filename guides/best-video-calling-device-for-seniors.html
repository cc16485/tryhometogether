The best video-calling device for seniors in 2026

An honest look at every real way to video call an aging parent, what each one gets right, where it falls apart, and how to choose based on your parent, not the marketing.

There's no single "best" device, there's the best fit for your parent. Someone who still texts and scrolls has different needs than someone with low vision, arthritis, or memory changes. Below are the four real options, sorted by how much they ask of the person receiving the call, because that's what decides whether the calls actually keep happening.

A closer look

★★☆☆☆

Smartphone (the one they have)

Free to try. With big text, high contrast, and a one-tap shortcut, a phone can work, if your parent can reliably find, unlock, hold, and aim it. For many older adults, that's exactly the chain that breaks. Great as a first attempt; often not the lasting answer.

★★★☆☆

Senior-focused tablet

~$100–$300 plus upkeep. Bigger buttons, simplified menus, and auto-answer for approved callers make these friendlier than a regular tablet. The catch is real life: it has to be charged, kept in reach, and not wandered off with. Charging and "where did I put it" are where these usually stall.

★★★☆☆

Smart display (voice assistant with a screen)

~$100–$250. "Call Sarah" out loud and it dials, no holding required, which is a genuine step up. But the screen is small, the setup and Wi-Fi troubleshooting fall to you, and the always-listening microphone makes some families uneasy. Good for a voice-comfortable senior.

Best for most families★★★★★

TV-based calling

~$99/month, all-in. Instead of a new device to learn, it uses the television your parent already turns on every day. The call appears on the big screen and can answer on its own, so there's nothing to find, charge, unlock, or hold. It's the only option that asks essentially nothing of the person receiving the call, which is why the calls actually keep happening. HomeTogether works this way, with a physical privacy shutter over the camera between calls and wellness check-in features included.

How to choose

  • Can they reliably unlock and hold a phone? If yes, start with simplifying the phone they own. If no, skip handhelds entirely.
  • Is vision or arthritis a factor? Bigger, no-hold options (smart display or TV) win.
  • Is memory a factor? Choose something that answers on its own and lives on a screen they already know, that points to TV-based calling.
  • Who maintains it? Be honest about charging, Wi-Fi, and updates. If that's on you from three states away, an all-in monthly service saves a lot of frustration.

Common questions

What's the best video-calling device for seniors?

For someone who struggles with handhelds, a TV-based option is usually best, because it removes the steps that make calls fail. Smartphones and tablets can suit more independent seniors. Match the tool to your parent's eyesight, dexterity, and memory.

How much does one cost?

Tablets and smart displays are often $100–$300 up front, with setup and upkeep on family. TV-based services like HomeTogether are about $99/month all-in, including the device and support, with no long contract.

Tablet or TV?

A TV is usually easier for vision, dexterity, or memory challenges, it's large, familiar, and can answer automatically. A tablet can work for a more independent senior but must be charged, found, and operated each time.
