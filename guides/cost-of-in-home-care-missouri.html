How much does in-home care cost in Missouri?

A plain, honest breakdown of what in-home caregivers cost across Missouri in 2026, and how to pay a fair rate without overpaying.

If you're arranging care for an aging parent or spouse, the first question is almost always the same: what will this cost? Here's a clear picture for Missouri, with real ranges and the choices that move the number up or down.

In-home care rates in Missouri (2026)

Costs depend on how you hire (through an agency or directly), the level of care, and where you live. Here are typical ranges:

Type of careTypical Missouri rate
Companion care (company, meals, errands)$18–$24 / hour
Personal care (bathing, dressing, mobility)$22–$30 / hour
Through an agency$25–$35 / hour
Hiring a caregiver directly$18–$26 / hour
Overnight care$150–$250 / night
Live-in care$200–$350 / day

Larger metros like St. Louis and Kansas City sit toward the higher end; smaller towns and rural areas often run lower.

The biggest cost lever: whether you go through an agency or hire directly. An agency handles everything but adds a markup, often $7–$12 an hour on top of the caregiver's pay. Hiring directly means more of your money goes to the person actually doing the caring.

Agency vs. hiring a caregiver directly

Hiring through an agency

The agency employs the caregiver, handles payroll and taxes, and covers call-offs. You pay for that convenience with a higher hourly rate, and you usually don't choose the specific person.

Hiring a caregiver directly

You choose the person yourself, agree on a rate, and pay them directly, so there's no markup. You take on hiring and scheduling, and you become a "household employer" for tax purposes above a yearly threshold (manageable, and often handled by a payroll service). The upside: lower cost, and a caregiver you picked.

What changes the price

  • Level of care. Companionship costs less than hands-on personal care or memory care.
  • Hours. A few hours a day costs far less than around-the-clock coverage.
  • Overnight vs. daytime. Overnight and live-in are usually priced differently from hourly daytime care.
  • Experience and certifications. A CNA or an experienced memory-care aide commands more, and is often worth it.
  • Location. Urban areas run higher than rural Missouri.

How to pay less without losing quality

  1. Hire directly. Removing the agency markup is the single biggest saving.
  2. Match the care level to the need. Don't pay personal-care rates for companionship.
  3. Be a great client. Reliable hours and fair, on-time pay keep a wonderful caregiver, and turnover is expensive.
  4. Vet well up front. A background check and a real interview cost little and prevent costly mistakes.

Common questions

How much does in-home care cost per hour in Missouri?

In 2026, expect roughly $20–$30 an hour through an agency, and about $18–$26 an hour hiring directly. Rates vary by location and the level of care.

Is it cheaper to hire a caregiver directly?

Usually yes, because there's no agency markup, so more of your money reaches the caregiver. You take on hiring and scheduling, but tools and payroll services make it manageable.

How much is 24-hour or live-in care?

Hourly around-the-clock care adds up fast (often $400+ a day). A flat live-in rate is commonly $200–$350 a day in Missouri, depending on the care and whether the caregiver sleeps at night.

Does Medicare pay for in-home caregivers?

Generally no. Medicare covers short-term skilled care after a hospital stay, not ongoing non-medical help like companionship or bathing. Most families pay privately, use long-term-care insurance, or explore Missouri Medicaid programs if eligible.

This guide is general information, not financial or legal advice. Rates are typical ranges and vary by situation. For household-employer tax questions, talk to a tax professional.
