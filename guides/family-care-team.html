Manage care successfully

Your family care team & emergency prep

No one should carry this alone. When the family divides the work clearly and prepares for the unexpected together, care gets steadier and the load gets lighter.

Divide the roles

Resentment usually grows when the work is invisible and unequal. Naming roles fixes a lot of that. You might split it as:

  • Primary contact, the caregiver's main point person and coordinator.
  • Medical, tracks appointments, medications, and talks with doctors.
  • Finances, handles pay, bills, and paperwork.
  • Scheduling, keeps the calendar and coverage sorted.
  • Hands-on, family who provide direct care or visits.

They don't have to be even, just clear. And they can flex over time.

Helping from far away

Distance doesn't mean helplessness. Long-distance family can own the roles that don't need to be local, finances, scheduling, research, phone calls, and appointments, and take real weight off whoever is nearby. Regular video calls keep them close, too. (That's exactly what HomeTogether TV is for.)

Prepare for an emergency, before one

  • Post emergency contacts and the doctor's number where the caregiver can see them.
  • Keep a current medication list and a one-page "about me" sheet (conditions, allergies, how they communicate).
  • Pack a simple hospital go-bag, medication list, ID and insurance info, a comfort item, and that about-me sheet.
  • Make sure everyone knows the plan, who to call, and in what order.
  • Have a backup care plan for when the caregiver can't come.
Do it on a calm day. Ten minutes of emergency prep now saves an hour of panic later. In a real emergency, always call 911 first.

Add trusted hands to your team

A dependable caregiver is the anchor of the whole plan. Find one your family trusts.
