Staying close

Getting a hesitant parent comfortable with video calls

"I don't do that computer stuff." If that's your parent, the resistance you're hitting almost always comes from fear, not stubbornness. Here's what's really going on, and how to make video calls feel safe instead of stressful.

You bought the tablet. You set up the app. And somehow, months later, it's still in the drawer. It's easy to feel frustrated, or to decide your parent just "won't try." But the truth underneath is usually much softer than that, and once you understand it, the whole thing gets easier.

Why they resist (it's rarely stubbornness)

  • Fear of looking foolish. No one likes feeling incompetent, least of all a parent in front of their own child. If a video call has ever gone wrong, they may quietly decide it's safer not to try.
  • Fear of breaking something expensive. To someone who didn't grow up with this, a screen full of buttons feels like a device where one wrong tap could ruin it.
  • Seeing their own face. Many older adults find it genuinely uncomfortable to watch themselves on screen during a call.
  • Too many steps. Find the device, charge it, unlock it, open the app, tap answer, hold it steady. Every step is another chance to feel lost.
The reframe that helps: your parent isn't refusing to see you. They're avoiding the feeling of failing at something in front of you. Take that feeling away, and the willingness is almost always there.

Gentle ways to lower the pressure

1

Start tiny and keep it light

First calls should be two minutes, with zero agenda. Say goodnight, show them the dog, wave. When calls are short and pleasant, they stop feeling like a test.

2

Never turn it into a lesson

The fastest way to end the experiment is to say "no, tap the other one." Praise what works, ignore the fumbles, and let it be about connection, not competence.

3

Keep everything identical every time

Same time of day, same way of answering, same face. Predictability is comforting. Surprises are what make people give up.

4

Remove the steps instead of teaching them

This is the real unlock. Rather than coaching your parent through a phone, use a setup where the call simply appears and answers itself. If there's nothing to do, there's nothing to get wrong, and nothing to be afraid of.

Common questions

Why does my parent refuse to video call?

Usually fear, not stubbornness: fear of looking foolish, of breaking something, or of too many confusing steps. Lower the pressure and remove the steps, and the resistance tends to fade.

How do I make my parent comfortable with video calls?

Keep first calls short and warm, never make it feel like a test, keep the routine identical, and use a device that answers on its own so there's nothing to learn.

What's the easiest setup for a technophobic senior?

A TV-based system like HomeTogether that brings the call to the television and answers automatically. No app, no phone, no button to find.
