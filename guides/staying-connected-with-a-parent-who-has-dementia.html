Caring from far away

Staying connected with a parent who has dementia

When memory changes, the connection doesn't have to. A familiar face, a steady routine, and a calm voice still reach them, often more than words do. Here's how to keep showing up, even from far away.

Dementia doesn't erase the need to feel loved and safe. It just changes how you reach it. Your parent may not recall your last visit, or what you talked about five minutes ago, but the warmth of seeing someone who clearly loves them lands anyway. That feeling is what you're staying connected for, and it's very much still reachable.

What helps most

1

Keep a rhythm

The same time each day or week becomes something they can feel even when they can't name it. Routine lowers anxiety. A short "good morning" call every day beats a long call once a month.

2

Let your face do the work

Words can slip away; faces and voices don't, not as fast. Smile, speak slowly, use their name and yours gently ("Hi Mom, it's Sarah"), and don't quiz them. Being seen and recognized matters more than the conversation making perfect sense.

3

Don't test, don't correct

Skip "Do you know who this is?" and don't argue over facts or dates. If they're in a different year or looking for someone long gone, meet them there kindly. The goal is comfort, not accuracy.

4

One voice at a time

A screen full of grandkids all talking at once can overwhelm. Take turns. Let the grandchildren wave and say one thing each. Calm and simple keeps them present with you.

The quiet truth about the technology: for a parent with dementia, any device they have to operate will eventually fail them, not because they don't try, but because unlocking a phone and finding an app is exactly the kind of multi-step task that gets harder. The connection has to work without them managing it.

Make the call effortless on their end

This is where most families get stuck. You want to see each other every day, but you can't ask someone with memory changes to reliably find, charge, unlock, and answer a phone. So the calls dwindle, and the guilt grows.

The way through is a call that they don't have to run. When the video simply appears on the television they already turn on, and can answer on its own, the whole burden lifts. They look up, and there's your face, no tapping, no holding, no "which button?" It becomes the easy, daily kind of contact that actually soothes.

Common questions

How do you stay connected with a parent who has dementia?

Routine and familiarity: same time, short calls, a warm face, and no memory quizzes. Consistency calms anxiety. A setup they don't have to operate, like a call on their TV, makes daily contact realistic.

Are video calls good for someone with dementia?

Yes, when they're effortless. A familiar face and voice ease loneliness and agitation. The key is removing effort on their end, no phone to unlock or hold.

What should you avoid on a call?

Don't test their memory, correct facts, rush, or have several people talk at once. Keep it calm, warm, and one voice at a time.
