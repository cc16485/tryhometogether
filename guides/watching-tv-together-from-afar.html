Staying close

Watching shows, church, and the game together from afar

The best video calls with a parent often aren't really "calls" at all. They're doing something together: the same show, the Sunday service, the ballgame. Sharing an experience feels warmer than one more "how are you feeling?" and it gives you both something to talk about.

Here's a small truth about staying connected: a check-in call can start to feel like an appointment, for both of you. But sitting together through a favorite program, or cheering the same team, is just being family. When you can't be on the same couch, you can still share the same moment, and that's what people remember.

Ways to actually do things together

1

Watch a show at the same time

Pick a program you both like, start a video call, and watch together while you chat about it, just like you would side by side. A weekly show becomes a standing date.

2

Bring them to church

For a parent who can no longer get to services, this one means the world. Many churches stream now, so they can watch worship on their TV, and you can join by video before and after so it feels shared rather than solitary.

3

Cheer the game together

Put the game on both ends and keep a call open. Half the fun of sports is reacting with someone. A grandparent and grandchild yelling at the same touchdown from two states away is a memory in the making.

4

Share a meal or a photo album

Eat dinner "together" at the same time, or slowly look through old family photos on the screen. Gentle, unhurried, and full of stories.

Why this matters more than it sounds: shared experiences give a lonely day texture and give your parent something to anticipate. And they take the pressure off, because you don't need anything to say. The show gives you plenty.

Common questions

How can I watch TV together with a parent far away?

Pick a show you both enjoy, start a video call at the same time, and watch while you chat, just like sitting together. It works best when they can see and hear you easily on a big screen.

How can a homebound parent attend church remotely?

Many churches stream services. Your parent can watch on their TV, and you can join by video before and after so it feels shared. Some TV systems include worship content too.

What are good activities to do over video with an elderly parent?

Watch a show or game, look through old photos, share a meal at the same time, or read to a grandchild. Doing something alongside them beats a status-check call.
