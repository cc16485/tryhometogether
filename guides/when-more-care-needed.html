When more care may be needed

Needs change, sometimes slowly, sometimes overnight. Noticing when the current arrangement isn't quite enough, and acting early, keeps your loved one safe and spares everyone a crisis.

Signs it may be time for more

  • More frequent falls, or near-misses
  • Missed medications, or confusion about them
  • Weight loss, skipped meals, or an empty fridge
  • Poor hygiene, or wearing the same clothes for days
  • Unsafe moments alone, wandering, leaving the stove on, getting lost
  • Increasing confusion, agitation, or withdrawal
  • The current caregiver, or you, is stretched too thin to keep up safely

Your options, from smaller to bigger

  • More hours with the current caregiver, if they're a good fit.
  • A second caregiver to cover more of the week, or nights.
  • An agency for reliable coverage or higher-acuity needs.
  • Skilled or medical care (a visiting nurse, therapy) if health needs have grown.
  • A higher level of care, assisted living or memory care, when home is no longer safe.

Having the conversation

These talks are hard. Lead with love and their wishes: "I want you to be safe and comfortable, let's figure this out together." Involve them in the decision as much as possible, and bring in the doctor for a clear-eyed look at needs. Deciding with someone, not for them, makes a change far easier to accept.

Trust the pattern, not one bad day. Everyone has off days. But a steady drift, more falls, more confusion, more missed basics, is your signal to adjust care before it becomes an emergency. When in doubt, ask the doctor.

Need to add or change care?

Whether it's more hours or a second caregiver, find someone you trust to step in, and keep a backup on your bench.
